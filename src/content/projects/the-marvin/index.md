## The brief

A product visualization course: take a product from idea to a convincing visual story. I wanted the object to carry a mood as well as a shape — mid-century modern design and the smoke-filled studio feel of Motown Records. Warm wood, polished metal, woven fabric and simple, honest forms.

## Two directions

Sketching centred on recorded music: studio and action mics, a remote, radios, speakers and a meeting mic. I annotated as I went so the reasoning wouldn't get lost. Two directions stood out.

**A — The Marvin.** A studio mic with a removable lav mic, good for both professional vocal recording and street interviews. Two mics in one body and a strong tie to the theme. The risk: many functions in a small body, and complex details to model.

**B — Self-playing turntable.** A record player with a built-in speaker that gives the illusion of the record playing itself. Iconic and playful, but a crowded category, and the built-in speaker would limit sound quality.

I chose A. It joined function and form in one object, and a quick clay model let me check the size in my hands before committing to CAD. The turntable was set aside — a strong idea, but the microphone had more left to explore.

## Detail design

Detailing focused on where the dial and display sit, and on a dock that holds the lavalier mic in the frame. An exploded view of the assembly guided the modelling of each part.

Textures were set up for contrast: a polished stainless steel frame and dial around a warm walnut front panel, topped with a thick 60s/70s knitted fabric pop filter.

## Visualization

The product is named after Marvin Gaye, and the renders borrow the earthy tones and swagger of his albums. Instead of a white background, the final images put the mic in a period recording studio, so the mood does as much work as the form.

The project ends in a one-minute product film: the materials up close, the assembly coming apart, the lav mic in use outdoors, and the EQ modes on the display.
